Chapter III: Optical Resonators and Laser Beam Propagation
==========================================================

Source: Gerrard and Burch, *Introduction to Matrix Methods in Optics* (1975),
Chapter III.

Illustrative problems
---------------------

.. rubric:: Problem 3.1 — Recovering an unknown system matrix from focal-power tests

Let the sealed system be :math:`M_0=(A_0,B_0;C_0,D_0)` and let weak lenses of
powers :math:`P_1` and :math:`P_2` be attachable at its input and output.
Because equivalent focal length measures :math:`C=-1/f`, the four tests give

.. math::

   C_1=C_0-D_0P_1,
   \qquad C_2=C_0-A_0P_2,

.. math::

   C_{12}=C_0-D_0P_1-A_0P_2+B_0P_1P_2.

Solving sequentially produces

.. math::

   \boxed{D_0=\frac{C_0-C_1}{P_1}},\qquad
   \boxed{A_0=\frac{C_0-C_2}{P_2}},

.. math::

   \boxed{B_0=
   \frac{C_{12}+C_0-C_1-C_2}{P_1P_2}}.

The independent check is :math:`A_0D_0-B_0C_0=1`; disagreement measures
experimental or paraxial-model error.

.. rubric:: Problem 3.2 — Sagittal separation and the optical invariant

Propagate both marginal ray vectors by the same translation matrix and solve
for the plane where their heights agree.  Use the paraxial sag relation
:math:`s\simeq y^2/(2R)` together with :math:`V=ny/R`.  After substituting the
intersection distance, all translation-dependent terms cancel and the
sagittal difference is one-half of the corresponding two-ray determinant
(with the book's reduced-coordinate normalization).

That determinant is the Lagrange optical invariant, so its survival is not an
accident: multiplication by any unimodular transfer matrix preserves it.
Replacing one marginal ray by its diametrically opposite ray reverses the
determinant and hence the sagittal ordering.  Physically, the wavefront that
led at one equal-area crossing lags at the other.

.. rubric:: Problem 3.3 — General round-trip matrix for a compound dye-laser cavity

Let :math:`M_0` transfer rays from the left mirror to the right mirror, and
represent each mirror by :math:`R(P_i)`.  Write the outward chain first, then
the reciprocal return chain with the refractive-index sign reversed as
prescribed in Chapter II.  Multiplying the two translations through the
intervening system and both mirror matrices yields the round-trip matrix at
the output mirror.

Two checks simplify the algebra:

* every factor is unimodular, hence :math:`\det M_{rt}=1`;
* a round trip referenced at one physical plane must have equal diagonal
  elements before the final output-mirror factor is applied.

Keeping the intermediate matrix in symbolic :math:`A_0,B_0,C_0,D_0` form is
the shortest route; expanding numerical lens trains first obscures both
checks.

.. rubric:: Problem 3.4 — Stability range and output-wave curvature

Write the round-trip matrix immediately before the output mirror as
:math:`(A_r,B_r;C_r,A_r)` and multiply by :math:`R(P_2)`.  The trace becomes
:math:`2A_r-B_rP_2`.  Stable operation requires its half-trace to lie strictly
between :math:`-1` and :math:`1`, so :math:`P_2` lies between the two endpoint
values

.. math::

   \boxed{P_2=\frac{2(A_r-1)}{B_r}}
   \quad\hbox{and}\quad
   \boxed{P_2=\frac{2(A_r+1)}{B_r}},

with their numerical order selected from the sign of :math:`B_r`.  For any
allowed value, the fixed-point solution for :math:`q` gives

.. math::

   \boxed{R_{\rm out}=\frac{2B}{D-A}=\frac{2}{P_2}},

so the oscillating wavefront matches the output mirror.  At either stability
endpoint the Gaussian solution becomes marginal.

.. rubric:: Problem 3.5 — Short single-frequency carbon-dioxide laser

A parallel output requires a plane output mirror.  With cavity length
:math:`L=0.5\ \mathrm m`, the round-trip trace condition
:math:`(A+D)/2=\cos1` gives an input-mirror power

.. math::

   \boxed{P_1=0.92\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}},\qquad
   \boxed{R_1=2/P_1=2.17\ \mathrm m}.

The resulting matrix is approximately

.. math::

   M_{rt}=\begin{bmatrix}0.54&0.77\\-0.92&0.54\end{bmatrix}.

At the carbon-dioxide laser wavelength, the Gaussian formulas give
:math:`w_0=1.76\ \mathrm{mm}` and :math:`z_0=0.915\ \mathrm m`.  The spot
radius at the curved mirror is :math:`1.14w_0`, so the maximum :math:`1/e^2`
diameter is about 4 mm.  Allowing diffraction, alignment, wall-reflection, and
discharge margins leads to the practical recommendation
:math:`\boxed{10\ \mathrm{mm}}` for the tube or limiting diaphragm diameter.

.. rubric:: Problem 3.6 — Internal-reflection focus inside a plano-convex lens

Use refraction matrices :math:`R_1,R_2`, internal-reflection matrices
:math:`R'_1,R'_2`, and the 1-cm glass translation :math:`T`.  Since the second
surface is plane, its refraction and reflection matrices are identities.  The
ray after successive internal reflections is therefore generated by repeated
application of :math:`TR'_1T`.

Progressively tracing the marginal ray shows that the first internal focus
after two reflections is

.. math::

   \boxed{0.909\ \mathrm{cm}\text{ left of the plane surface}},

about 1 mm inside the curved front surface.  Each uncoated glass-air
reflection retains approximately 0.04 of the incident power, but the Airy
spot area is much smaller than the original 1-cm-radius beam.  For a He--Ne
wavelength, the average focal irradiance is therefore approximately

.. math::

   \boxed{710\text{ times the incident irradiance}}.

This large value explains the internal-damage warning; aberration and a
longer wavelength reduce it, but do not remove the hazard.
